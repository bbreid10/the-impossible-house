# V8 design guide · screens and all six chapters

## Look and feel

Gothic architectural printmaking meets refined escape-room tabletop props. Restrained charcoal, worn brass, weathered parchment, ink, and candlelit diorama photography. No candy colors, glossy gradients, 3D toy buttons, floating sparkles, or obviously synthetic portraits. Room art changes with the chapter and uses a consistent hand-authored SVG style. Characters remain highly customizable and recognizable at map scale.

## Screens

- **Landing:** One sentence explains the premise. Two major calls to action (solo story and friends), then unobtrusive customization, help and chapter test controls. No minigame shortcuts.
- **Character creator:** Persistent name and multiple hair, skin, outfit and accessory options. Adult wardrobe, clean swatches and one clear save button.
- **Online lobby:** Private/public access, room code, copyable invitation, Ready states, 2–4 guests, chat and host start.
- **Story map:** Chapter status, currently controlled explorer, one-step objective, 2×3 floor map, large rotation dock immediately beneath it, and an illustrated rulebook. Door alignments and changed rooms are visibly marked.
- **Rulebook:** Sentence fragments must be collected and inscribed before casting. Every law lists the rooms it will affect and whether its magical passages are active. The reality banner makes rule casting legible.
- **Journal and optional puzzle:** Shared clue notes, reflection glimpses, optional torn-page word hunts that start within the chapter itself, one server-wide timer and collaborative finds.
- **End of chapter:** Stars, moves, rotations and hints. Host proceeds to the next stage, with no skipped main story.

## Chapter pacing and purpose

1. **The House Has Opinions:** Teaches switching explorers, the crank, turning a room, collecting two word cards, turning mirrors into a physical escape route, holding two plates and finding Room C. Tutorial focuses on one move at a time rather than revealing a lengthy walkthrough.
2. **The Clockwork Gallery:** Introduces gears that share rotation and create a direct walkable shaft when the new law is cast. Players explore for word fragments and must coordinate at the twin cog-room seals. Optional torn-page team hunt in the Gear Gallery.
3. **The Forgotten Wing:** Blocked passages become real corridors (two marked edges), and the brass orrery requires interpreting three poetic clues and aligning Star–Clock–Moon before the escape opens.
4. **The Portrait Parliament:** Compare separate clue notes and deduce which symbol belongs between two others. An unlocked mirror law can be used for remote clue reconnaissance, making choosing the right law part of the strategy.
5. **The Borrowed Hour:** Decode clue order, operate linked gears and touch Moon, Clock and Sun runes in sequence. Wrong guesses reset the runes without failing the chapter.
6. **The Heart of the House:** Combining all previously earned laws is mandatory. Mirrors free a stranded explorer, linked gears make the heart ring and wall magic opens the real exit. An optional last guestbook word hunt is in Room A.

## What to look for in public testing

Can somebody understand that Room C is the exit within 10 seconds? Can they deliberately complete Chapter 1 without an outside walkthrough? Can they predict exactly which rooms a rule cast will change? Can two distant players solve the same timed page concurrently? Does the visual design feel mature, not toy-like? Ask which mechanic made them feel clever and where the first confusion occurred.
